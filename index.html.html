Chapter 01 · Foundations

How a machine learns to talk

The concepts every other chapter stands on: what AI, machine learning, deep learning, generative AI and LLMs are, how a model is trained, and what happens inside the model when it answers.

AI vs ML vs DLTrainingTransformersTokensTemperatureEmbeddings

The apprentice who read everything

Picture an apprentice locked inside the world's largest library. Nobody teaches grammar or facts. The apprentice plays one game, billions of times: cover the next word, guess it, check, adjust. Slowly, patterns emerge: grammar, facts, style, even reasoning. Then teachers arrive to show good manners: how to be helpful, honest and safe. That apprentice is a large language model.

AI is a family of nested ideas

Biggest circle

Artificial intelligence

Any technique that lets machines do tasks that normally need human intelligence. Includes old-style hand-written rules.

Machine learning (ML)

Instead of writing rules, we show the system examples and it learns the patterns itself.

Deep learning (DL)

Machine learning with many-layered neural networks. Great with messy data: images, sound, language.

Generative AI

Deep-learning models that create new content: text, images, audio, video, code.

Smallest circle

Large language model (LLM)

A generative model trained on huge amounts of text to predict the next piece of text. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini run on LLMs.

Predictive (traditional) AIGenerative AI
JobClassify or predict: yes/no, a score, a categoryCreate something new: a paragraph, an image, code
OutputA label or numberOpen-ended content
Everyday exampleSpam filter, face unlock, “you may also like”Drafting an email, summarizing a report, making a picture

Four ways a machine learns from data

With answers

Supervised

Learn from labelled examples. 10,000 photos marked “cat” or “dog” teach the model to tell them apart.

No answers

Unsupervised

Find hidden groups in unlabelled data, such as grouping songs that sound alike.

Trial and reward

Reinforcement

Try an action, get a reward or penalty, improve. This is how AI learned to beat Go champions.

How LLMs start

Self-supervised

Hide part of the data and predict it. The text itself supplies the answer, so no human labelling is needed.

Key idea: traditional programming is “rules + data → answers”. Machine learning flips it: “data + answers → rules”. The learned rules live inside the model as numbers.

A neural network is a huge stack of adjustable dials

Neurons and layers

Each circle does a tiny calculation. Early layers spot simple patterns (edges, letters); deeper layers combine them into ideas (faces, meaning).

Weights = the dials

Every line has a strength called a weight. Learning means adjusting weights. Together these are the model's parameters; large models have billions of them.

Deep = many layers

“Deep learning” simply means many hidden layers stacked together.

The transformer: why modern AI took off

Since 2017

Attention

The transformer architecture lets every word look at every other word at once and decide which ones matter. That is how the model knows which “bank” you mean. Transformers also train well on many chips in parallel, which made giant models possible. The “T” in GPT stands for transformer.

Learning vs using

Training ≠ inference

Training happens once and costs a fortune. Inference is each time you send a message. During inference the weights are frozen.

Common myth

It doesn't learn from your chat

The model itself does not change while you talk to it. “Memory” features save notes outside the model and feed them back in later.

Time limit

Knowledge cutoff

A model only knows what was in its training data up to a date. For anything newer it needs web search or your documents.

Rule of thumb: one token is about 4 characters, or three-quarters of an English word. Pricing, speed and context limits are all counted in tokens. (Numbers shown are illustrative.)

Context window: the model's desk

The maximum tokens the model can consider at once: instructions, history, documents and its own reply. Modern models range from tens of thousands to over a million tokens. When the desk is full, older material falls off or must be summarized.

Embeddings: turning meaning into a map

What it is

An embedding is a list of numbers (often hundreds or thousands) that captures what a piece of text means. Similar meanings get similar numbers, so they sit close together on the map.

Why it matters

Search by meaning, not exact words. “Kitten” finds documents about cats even if the word “kitten” never appears.

Where you'll meet it

RAG search (Chapter 03), recommendations, grouping similar tickets, finding duplicates. Stored in a vector database.

Two kinds of numbers, don't mix them up: token IDs are just labels for text pieces. Embeddings are rich coordinates that carry meaning.

A model is an engine. A product is the whole car.

When you use ChatGPT or Claude, you are using an application wrapped around a model. Every enterprise solution in the next chapters is built from the same five layers.

InterfaceChat, voice, mobile app, contact centre, a button inside another tool
Application logicRouting, memory, safety checks, approvals, logging
Tools and dataSearch, documents, company systems, code execution
InstructionsSystem prompt, settings (temperature, output format), examples

The golden rule: the model sees only what the application places on its desk. It never opens databases by itself. Every connection to company systems passes through controlled application code.
Chapter 02 · Virtual assistant

The front desk: understand and route

A virtual assistant listens, works out what you need (your intent), and sends you to the right place: an answer, a live system, or a person. It is the front door of most AI solutions.

IntentEntitiesRoutingHandoffContainment

Sara at 11 pm

Sara's parked car was hit last week. Tonight she opens her insurer's app and types “any update on my claim?” The assistant does not need to know everything. It needs to recognize her, understand that she wants a status update, find the right claim, and fetch the answer from the claims system. When she later types “I disagree with the decision”, it knows that is a different need and brings in a person.

Use case · Insurance

Claim-status assistant

  1. Sara types any update on my claim? She is already logged in.
  2. Intent detected: claim status (high confidence). Entity: her one open claim is found on her account.
  3. Route B: the assistant calls a read-only claim-status service.
  4. Reply: the claim is with the adjuster; the repair estimate is the next milestone.
  5. Sara types I disagree with the decision. New intent: dispute. Route C: a claims specialist receives the full conversation summary.
Lesson: same topic, different intent, different route. Getting intent right is the assistant's most important job.

Chapter 03 · Retrieval-augmented generation

The librarian: find first, then answer

RAG turns a closed-book exam into an open-book exam. Before the model answers, the system searches your approved documents and places the best passages on the model's desk, so the answer comes from evidence instead of memory.

ChunkingMetadataVector searchRerankingGroundednessCitations

Omar and the expired passport

Omar works in compliance at a payments company. A new customer in Ontario offers a passport that expired last month. Can the team accept it? A general chatbot would guess. A RAG assistant behaves like a careful librarian: it searches only the approved policy library, pulls the clause for the right country and the current date, answers with a citation, and admits it when the library has no clear answer.

Use case · Fintech compliance (illustrative)

KYC policy assistant

  1. Omar asks: Can we verify identity with a passport that expired last month?
  2. Query rewritten to “accepted identity documents, expired passport, Canada”.
  3. Filters keep only Canadian KYC policy, current version, compliance-team access.
  4. Hybrid search finds 12 passages; the reranker keeps the top 3.
  5. The answer cites the clause on accepted documents and lists the permitted alternative methods.
  6. If two policy versions disagree, the assistant flags the conflict instead of picking one.
Lesson: a good RAG answer is relevant, grounded, current and permitted. A great match from an outdated or restricted document is still a wrong answer.

Three numbers that tell you if RAG works

Retrieval relevance

Did the search bring back the right passages?

Groundedness (faithfulness)

Is every claim in the answer supported by those passages?

Freshness and access

Is the source current, and allowed for this person?

Modern variations

Agentic RAG

An agent decides when to search, what to search, and searches again if the first results are weak.

GraphRAG

Adds a knowledge graph so the system can follow relationships (who owns what, what depends on what).

RAG or fine-tuning?

RAG for knowledge that changes. Fine-tuning for behaviour, tone or format that stays the same.

Chapter 04 · Agentic AI

The worker, and then the team

An agent is given a goal rather than a single question. It plans, uses approved tools, reads the results, checks its own progress, and keeps going until the job is done or a limit is reached.

Priya's $75 surprise

Priya's phone bill jumped by $75 after a trip abroad. She asks for help. An agent takes the goal “resolve this dispute” and works like a careful new employee: read the plan's roaming rules, pull the usage records, check whether a credit is allowed, ask a supervisor if the amount is above its limit, apply the credit, confirm it went through, and explain everything to Priya.

One boss assigns and combines.

Pipeline

Each agent hands off to the next in order.

Parallel

Critic

Use several agents only when the work splits cleanly. More agents mean more cost, more handoffs, and more chances to disagree or repeat work.

How much freedom? The autonomy dial

Match autonomy to risk. Most enterprise agents live at level 2 or 3.

Level 1
Suggest

Drafts an action; a person does it.

Level 2
Act with approval

Prepares and waits for a yes.

Level 3
Act within limits

Acts alone below a threshold, escalates above it.

Level 4
Fully autonomous

Rare. Only for low-risk, reversible work.

Workflow or agent? A workflow follows a path coded in advance, with AI inside some steps. An agent chooses its own path. Many successful “agentic” solutions are mostly workflows, and that is usually a good thing: predictable, cheaper, easier to test.
Chapter 05 · Loops

Internal and external loops: how AI checks and improves its work

A loop is a cycle that repeats until a goal or a limit is reached. Reliable AI systems run two kinds: a fast internal loop inside each task, and a slow external loop around the whole system.

Agent loop (ReAct)Self-correctionHuman-in-the-loopFeedback and evalsStop conditions

Measure twice, cut once

A carpenter measures, cuts, checks the fit, and trims again. That is the fast loop inside the job. At month-end, the workshop owner studies returned furniture and changes how everyone works. That is the slow loop around the job. AI works the same way: seconds-long loops with no person involved, and weeks-long loops where people, data and tests make the whole system better.

Inner ring · seconds · no human

Internal loop

Inside a single task. The agent thinks, calls a tool, reads the result, checks it, and decides: done, try again, or try something else.

Outer ring · days to weeks · people + data

External loop

Around the system. Real usage is monitored, people give feedback, test suites run, and the team improves prompts, documents, tools or models before the next release.

Three internal loops

Think before answering

Reasoning loop

Reasoning models draft a hidden chain of thought, spot gaps, and revise before replying. You switch it on as “thinking” or “reasoning” mode.

ReAct pattern

Agent tool loop

Reason → Act (call a tool) → Observe the result → repeat. A failed lookup becomes a retry or a different tool.

Generator + critic

Self-correction loop

One step produces, another checks (format, facts, rules). If the check fails, the output is fixed and checked again.

Four external loops

Before

Human-in-the-loop

A person approves before a risky action happens.

During

Human-on-the-loop

A person monitors live activity and can step in.

After

Feedback and eval loop

Failures become test cases; fixes are proven before release.

Over time

Learning loop

Update documents, tools, prompts, or fine-tune a model with approved data.

Chapter 07 · Safety and quality

Trust is earned, then checked

Most controls sit around the model, not inside it. They check what goes in, what comes out, and what the system is allowed to touch.

GuardrailsEvalsHallucination stackTemplates

Day one at a new job

A new employee does not get the keys to the finance system on day one. They get training, a supervisor, clear rules and spending limits, and their early work is checked. Over time, as they prove themselves, they get more freedom. AI systems deserve exactly the same treatment.

The hallucination-reduction stack

No single setting fixes hallucination. Layers do.

1 · Before the model

Good inputs

Current approved sources, clean chunks, permission filters, hybrid search, reranking.

2 · At the call

Clear rules

Explicit instructions, evidence, structured output, “say I don't know” permission, suitable temperature.

3 · After generation

Check the output

Citations, factual support, policy rules, tool parameters, action results.

4 · Across releases

Keep testing

Regression evals, trace reviews, every real failure added to the test set.
